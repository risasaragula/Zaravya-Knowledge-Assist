CHEFDESK POS AND BILLING
1. What Is POS?
POS stands for Point of Sale.
A POS system is the software used to record customer orders, calculate bills and process payments.
In a restaurant, the POS can connect the order-taking process with billing, kitchen operations and other systems.
2. ChefDesk POS
ChefDesk provides restaurant POS functionality for dine-in, takeaway and delivery operations.
The system supports:
•	Counter billing
•	Tableside billing
•	Order management
•	Multiple payment methods
•	Split billing
•	KOT management
•	Table management
•	Modifiers and variants
•	QR ordering
•	Self-service ordering
•	Token management
ChefDesk describes its POS as touch-optimized and designed for restaurant operations. 
3. Payment Methods
ChefDesk supports payment methods such as:
•	Cash
•	Cards
•	UPI
•	Wallets
•	Split payments
Payment integrations allow restaurants to connect transactions with the broader billing process. 
4. GST Billing
ChefDesk provides GST-ready billing functionality.
The platform supports:
•	GST calculations
•	GST invoices
•	HSN information
•	Item-wise GST rates
•	GST-related reports
This is particularly relevant for restaurants operating in India. 
5. Offline Operation
ChefDesk supports offline operation.
If the internet connection becomes unavailable, billing can continue locally. Once connectivity is restored, the data can synchronize with the cloud.
This is useful for restaurants because billing does not necessarily have to stop when there is a temporary internet outage.

KITCHEN OPERATIONS
1. Kitchen Order Tickets
A KOT, or Kitchen Order Ticket, communicates customer orders to the kitchen.
Instead of restaurant staff verbally communicating every order, the POS can send order information to the appropriate kitchen station.
ChefDesk supports KOT management and kitchen routing. 
2. Kitchen Display System
A Kitchen Display System (KDS) is a digital system that displays orders to kitchen staff.
ChefDesk's KDS functionality can provide:
•	Real-time order display
•	Kitchen station routing
•	Item readiness
•	Preparation timers
•	Delay alerts
•	Batch preparation views
The objective is to help restaurant staff understand what needs to be prepared and where each order should be processed. 
3. Station Routing
Different restaurants may have different kitchen stations.
For example:
•	Grill
•	Bakery
•	Beverage
•	Main kitchen
•	Dessert
ChefDesk can route relevant items to appropriate stations.
This helps prevent kitchen staff from having to manually identify which items belong to their station.
4. KOT and KDS Difference
KOT: A kitchen order ticket communicates what has been ordered.
KDS: A kitchen display system digitally displays and manages those orders.
5. Example
A customer orders:
•	Chicken Biryani
•	Coke
•	Gulab Jamun
The restaurant's system can route the relevant items to the appropriate kitchen or preparation stations.
The kitchen can then see the order and track preparation.

INVENTORY AND FOOD COST MANAGEMENT
1. Inventory Management
Restaurant inventory includes ingredients, raw materials, packaging materials and other items required to operate the business.
ChefDesk provides inventory management functionality designed to connect restaurant sales with ingredient consumption. 
2. Recipe-Linked Inventory
ChefDesk can connect recipes with inventory ingredients.
For example, imagine a restaurant has a recipe for biryani containing:
•	Rice
•	Chicken
•	Oil
•	Spices
When the restaurant sells the dish, the corresponding ingredients can be deducted according to the configured recipe.
This helps restaurants understand theoretical ingredient consumption.
3. Stock Tracking
The system provides real-time stock tracking and can provide low-stock alerts.
Restaurants can configure minimum stock levels and monitor ingredient availability. 
4. Purchasing
Inventory functionality also covers procurement activities.
Features include:
•	Purchase orders
•	Goods Receipt Notes (GRN)
•	Vendor management
•	Vendor price history
•	Purchase returns
•	Stock transfers
5. Wastage
Food wastage can affect restaurant profitability.
ChefDesk allows wastage to be recorded according to reason and analyzed as part of inventory management.
Possible wastage reasons could include:
•	Spoilage
•	Preparation waste
•	Overproduction
•	Damaged stock
6. Food Cost
Food cost represents the cost associated with ingredients used to produce menu items.
Recipe-linked inventory allows restaurants to calculate food costs and identify differences between expected and actual consumption.
